What TRIM-edicine is

Turning the body's own repair signal into medicine

TRIM-edicine is a privately held biotechnology company developing novel biopharmaceutical products for medical needs that remain largely unmet: oral healthcare, dermal wounds, ischemia and reperfusion injury, sports medicine, and muscular dystrophy. At its core sits a small family of proteins with unusual biological properties, each one acting on cellular pathways that go wrong in disease.

Research and development at TRIM-edicine is focused on building the proof of concept behind each protein platform, then pairing that evidence with partners across the biomedical field who can carry it toward patients. As of 2015, the company holds global patent protection on MG53, covering both composition and use.

The proteins

Three platforms, one shared biology

TRIM-edicine builds around proteins that already do the work of repair inside the body. Each one is being developed toward a different unmet need.

TRIM72 · tissue damage

MG53

Mitsugumin53

MG53 is the first known essential molecular component of the cell's own membrane-repair process. When a membrane is injured and the oxidative outside world gets in, MG53 gathers into a vesicle and moves straight to the wound site to help reseal it. Beyond that first response, it also appears to calm the inflammation that chronic injuries leave behind. In animal studies, spanning mouse, pig, and monkey models, MG53 has supported repair across the brain, heart, lung, kidney, liver, and eye.

99%+ purity at scale · targeting IND submissions in the US and China
Synaptophysin family · muscle aging

MG29

Mitsugumin29

MG29 is a skeletal-muscle protein needed for normal muscle function. Mice without it develop a fragmented sarcoplasmic reticulum, swelling in the transverse tubules, and irregular calcium signaling under stress, the same pattern seen in naturally aging muscle. That overlap suggests falling MG29 levels may be part of how muscle ages, and that restoring it could help.

Preclinical · aging-related muscle disorders
Peptide therapeutic · oncology

ATAP

Apoptosis-targeting peptide

ATAP works through tail-anchoring domains that control mitochondrial membrane permeability, pushing cancer cells toward programmed death. The lead candidate, ATAP-M8, completed preclinical studies and received class 1 innovative drug candidate designation in China, beginning Phase I trials with late-stage cancer patients in March 2022.

Phase I · late-stage cancers, per company records
The platform

Three ways to apply the same biology

Rather than a single drug, TRIM-edicine develops three parallel technology platforms, each suited to a different kind of unmet need.

Protein therapeutics

Recombinant protein delivered directly, aimed at tissue repair in chronic disease and traumatic injury.

Regenerative biologics

Approaches for genetic disorders in which cells lose their normal capacity for plasticity and self-repair.

Bioactive peptides

Peptide fragments developed for pharmaceutical and cosmeceutical applications.
